KARGO HIRING RUBRIC
Derived from the 8 past-hire profiles (hires/) and the outcomes table in the case brief.
NOT derived from the Product Manager / Senior Product Manager job descriptions.

===============================================================
SOURCE PATTERNS (what separates Exceeds Expectations from Meets/Below)
===============================================================

Rated Exceeds Expectations: Rohan Desai (Head of Engineering), Sunita Krishnamurthy (Operations Lead),
Aditya Shetty (Sales Lead), Meghna Tiwari (Customer Success Manager), Lavanya Iyer (Product Manager)

Rated Meets Expectations: Vikram Nair (Product Manager), Rahul Bose (Growth & Marketing Lead)
Rated Below Expectations: Preetham Rao (Backend Engineer)

Pattern 1 - Unprompted, durable ownership.
Every Exceeds hire has at least one instance of noticing a gap nobody assigned them and building a
fix on their own initiative - and that fix outlived the task, becoming something others relied on
afterward, not a one-off personal accomplishment.
  - Rohan: built a shipment-tracking system in Excel when none existed - "adopted across the
    12-member operations team within two weeks"; built a Bill of Lading verification module
    unprompted - "30 colleagues using it within the first month; now a core platform feature."
  - Sunita: redesigned the team's documentation workflow overnight after a vendor changed formats
    without notice - "the updated process was retained permanently by the team."
  - Meghna: built the CS team's 30-60-90 onboarding framework - "the full CS team now uses"; wrote
    the onboarding checklist that "became the team's standard."
  - Aditya: built the company's first case study programme - "now used as primary sales collateral
    by the full team."
  - Lavanya: wrote the product's first formal API documentation, cutting integration timelines.
Contrast: Vikram's process improvements (a PRD template) were adopted only within his own 4-person
PM team, not an account or the org; most of his listed wins are metrics against an existing role,
not gaps he found and filled. Preetham's fixes (a monitoring dashboard, an indexing redesign) solved
problems for himself inside Cartexa's already-mature, well-resourced engineering org - nothing in his
profile becomes a practice other people now follow.

Pattern 2 - Sole ownership with no safety net, and failure turned into institutional memory.
Every Exceeds hire operates as the last point of accountability on something consequential - no
senior layer catches their call - and treats a mistake or crisis as something to document and fix
at the root, not just move past.
  - Aditya: "runs the full enterprise sales cycle independently... no account manager layer between
    the client and execution"; ran a post-mortem on a lost deal that is "now standard practice
    before account qualification."
  - Meghna: resolved a customs-hold crisis alone at 7pm "before the client became aware"; "no
    escalation to management in the last 14 months."
  - Lavanya: "sole PM"; drafted the internal and customer-facing post-mortem after a platform outage
    and "owned the follow-up action items to closure." Direct quote from her engineering lead: "the
    first PM here who has made calls we trusted immediately. She doesn't hedge."
  - Sunita: "comfortable owning processes start to finish with limited oversight"; ran a compliance
    audit and closed gaps before an inspection, unprompted.
Contrast: Vikram's decisions run through more layers (quarterly presentations to CTO/VP Sales,
a 4-person PM team) and his profile has no failure-into-practice example. Rahul's profile is
entirely wins and targets hit (quota, CAC, pipeline) with no instance of a documented mistake
becoming a lasting fix. Preetham participates in an on-call rotation and review process that
already existed at Cartexa - he never appears as the one who had to install it or who owned a
crisis end to end.

===============================================================
PRODUCT MANAGER - RUBRIC
===============================================================

Criterion name: Self-Started Fixes
What a strong candidate looks like: Their history shows at least one clear case of noticing a
problem nobody assigned them and building something to close it without being asked - even a small
one - not just executing a roadmap or backlog someone else defined.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Built to Last
What a strong candidate looks like: At least one thing they built or changed is described as being
used, kept, or relied on by other people afterward (a teammate, a client, a team) - not just
something they personally used once and moved on from.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Owns the Call Alone
What a strong candidate looks like: At least one instance where they made a consequential call or
handled a crisis end-to-end without a manager or senior layer stepping in to catch or approve it
first.
Weight: 25%

Criterion name: Failure Becomes Practice
What a strong candidate looks like: At least one instance of writing up or reflecting on a mistake,
loss, or gap - not just listing wins - and turning it into a concrete change (a new step, a doc, a
fix) rather than a vague lesson-learned statement.
Weight: 25%

===============================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER - RUBRIC
(same four criteria; bar raised, especially on independence)
===============================================================

Criterion name: Self-Started Fixes
What a strong candidate looks like: This shows up more than once, across more than one role or
company - a pattern of initiative, not a single anecdote from early in their career.
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Built to Last
What a strong candidate looks like: What they built is explicitly described as adopted, retained, or
made standard practice by a team, account, or company - language like "the full team now uses this"
or "retained permanently" - not just "I used this and it helped."
Weight: 20%

Criterion name: Owns the Call Alone
What a strong candidate looks like: Sole ownership without a senior layer above them is the norm in
their role, not an exception they can point to once - and ideally there is direct evidence (a quote,
a review note) that colleagues trusted their judgment on calls with real consequences.
Weight: 35%

Criterion name: Failure Becomes Practice
What a strong candidate looks like: The write-up of a mistake or crisis demonstrably changed how the
team or company operates afterward ("now standard practice," a retained process) - not just a
personal takeaway with no lasting artifact.
Weight: 25%
